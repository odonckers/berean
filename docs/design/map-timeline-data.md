# Map and timeline data

**Status:** Draft by Atlas. Echo reviews the data shape; schema and data-model sign-off is Wren's call. Numbers are proposals.
**Board:** "Atlas: map + timeline dataset schema and pilot dataset" (projects/5). It feeds the Wren row "Study tool: interactive Bible map + timeline".

## Goal

A small, cited dataset that the core can ship offline. With it, the map and timeline can show where and when an account happened, how sure we are, and which publication says so. Each fact traces back to a wol.jw.org page. Coordinates come from an outside gazetteer only when they agree with the publications.

## Files

Four JSON files, one per entity type. Each has a top-level `version`.

| File | Holds |
|---|---|
| `places.json` | cities, ports, islands, regions, capes, and so on |
| `people.json` | people who appear in the account |
| `periods.json` | named spans of time (for example, Paul's 2nd missionary tour), each with an ordered `route_gl` |
| `events.json` | outline-level legs and stops, each with a scripture, place, person, and period links |

This PR commits the four files at `core/berean-core/data/atlas/`. Nothing reads them yet: this PR has no Rust, Swift, or Cargo changes. The Rust side is not written yet (see Decisions).

JSON, not TSV, because entities are nested (citations, alternate sites). `serde_json` is already a dependency.

## Decisions (from Echo's review)

Rust types and FFI shape remain Wren's call.

- **Years** are signed integers (`i32`). CE is positive, BCE negative, and there is no year 0.
- **Path.** The files live inside the crate at `core/berean-core/data/atlas/`, so the crate builds on its own. The loader (not written yet) will compile them in with `include_str!("../data/atlas/places.json")` (and the same for the other three) and parse them once through `OnceLock`. That is the `languages.rs` pattern: `core/berean-core/src/languages.tsv` sits next to `languages.rs`, is loaded with `include_str!("languages.tsv")`, and is parsed once through `OnceLock`.
- **Commit, don't generate.** The committed JSON is the source of truth. Build and CI need no network.

### Generator (proposed future work)

`core/scripts/gen-atlas.py` is **not part of this PR**. It would be an optional offline check, run by hand like `gen-languages.py`, not part of build or CI. It would use the Python stdlib and public sources only, and have two modes:

- build: rebuilds the four files from WOL pages and gazetteer dumps.
- `--check`: re-verifies that each quote is a verbatim substring of the cited WOL page and at most 25 words, and validates every id reference.

The pilot files were generated by Atlas's local scripts, which run the same checks.

## Tests (for whoever writes the core loader)

Described here, not implemented in this PR. Same style as `ids_and_codes_are_unique` in `languages.rs`.

- All four files parse and share one `version`.
- Ids are unique and follow `<kind>:<slug>`.
- Every reference resolves.
- `year: 0` is rejected.
- `start <= end` for every date and every `scripture_ref`.
- Every event with basis "inherited from period" matches its period's dates exactly.
- Every `scripture_ref` parses, and lies inside its period's range.

## IDs

`<kind>:<slug>`, for example `place:antioch-pisidia`, `person:john-mark`, `period:paul-trip-to-rome`, `event:acts-16-11-15`. Slugs are stable and English, and use the NWT spelling. When two places share a name, the slug says which one (`antioch-syria` vs `antioch-pisidia`).

## Names

`names.nwt` is the NWT rendering. `names.alternate` lists other spellings and modern names that the publications use (Melita, Prisca, Kerti Hüyük). Localization later: `names.nwt` becomes a per-language map. Out of scope here.

## Places

```json
{ "id": "place:derbe", "type": "city",
  "names": {"nwt": "Derbe", "alternate": ["Kerti Hüyük"]},
  "identification": {"confidence": "established"},
  "coordinates": {"lat": 37.34921, "lon": 33.36178, "source": "Pleiades", "source_id": "648620",
    "source_url": "…", "precision": "precise",
    "cross_check": {"source": "OpenBible.info …", "distance_km": 0.1},
    "consistency": {"publication_says": "100 km SE of iconium", "gazetteer_gives_km": 96.2, "consistent": true}},
  "alternate_sites": [ … ], "citations": [ … ], "notes": [ … ] }
```

- `coordinates` is `null` unless a gazetteer point exists and is consistent with the publications. A point that fails the check moves to `rejected_coordinates`, so a reviewer can see it, and the app never draws it.
- `precision` is Pleiades' `locationPrecision` (`precise`, `rough`, `related`). Islands use their representative point, except Sicily: its `coordinates` is `null` because there is no Insight entry to check a point against (it is sourced only from map labels). Regions have no coordinates in this pilot (see open questions).
- `consistency` is computed. When Insight gives "X km DIR of Y", the gazetteer distance must be within 30% (or 5 km), and the bearing within 35° of DIR.

### Confidence (mechanical)

| Value | Rule |
|---|---|
| `established` | The publication names the site, or the modern place or ruins, without hedging. |
| `likely` | The publication hedges ("usually/generally identified", "thought", "believed"), or locates the place only by distance. |
| `uncertain` | The publication names competing sites, or gives no location. |

Competing sites go in `alternate_sites[]`, each with its own citations. A site the publication rejects has `"status": "rejected"`. Gazetteer-only alternatives carry a note saying so.

## Periods and events

Dates: `{"start": {"year": 49, "approx": true}, "end": {…}, "confidence": "likely", "basis": "cited" | "inherited from period"}`. Years are `i32`: CE positive, BCE negative, no year 0. "c." or "about" sets `approx`. `approx` is currently true on every date. It is kept for future exact dates and carries no information yet. An event with no date of its own inherits its period's date, and says so in `basis`.

Events take their titles and ranges from the NWT study edition's Acts outline. `sequence` is their order in Acts. `type` is `leg` (movement) or `stop`.

`event.periods` is a list so that an event can sit in two overlapping periods (for example, Ac 18:22 ends trip 2 and opens trip 3). Today no event has more than one.

### Scripture references

Every period and event has a free-text `scripture` and, next to it, a structured `scripture_ref`:

```json
"scripture": "Ac 17:22b-31",
"scripture_ref": {"book": 44, "book_name": "Acts",
  "start": {"chapter": 17, "verse": 22, "part": "b"}, "end": {"chapter": 17, "verse": 31}}
```

- `book` is the NWT book number (Acts = 44, as in the WOL `nwtsty/44` URLs).
- `part` (`"a"` or `"b"`) is optional and marks a half verse.
- A single verse has `start` == `end`.
- `scripture` keeps the WOL style: "-" within a chapter, "–" across chapters, ", " for two consecutive verses.
- Round trip: `scripture` parses to `scripture_ref`, and `scripture_ref` formats back to exactly `scripture`. All 52 strings in the pilot do.

### Custody in Caesarea

`period:paul-arrest-and-custody-in-caesarea` (type `custody`) covers Ac 21:27–26:32, c. 56-58 C.E., confidence `likely`. Sources:

- Insight ["Felix"](https://wol.jw.org/en/wol/d/r1/lp-e/1200001502) (it-1 p. 819): Felix "held Paul prisoner for two years after Paul’s last visit to Jerusalem, in about 56 C.E." It dates Festus "Perhaps in 58 C.E."
- Insight ["Paul"](https://wol.jw.org/en/wol/d/r1/lp-e/1200003406): Felix "kept Paul in custody for two years", and Paul was "sent to Rome in about 58 C.E."
- Insight ["Festus"](https://wol.jw.org/en/wol/d/r1/lp-e/1200001512): 58 C.E. "seems to be the most likely date of Festus’ accession as governor of Judea." The year "is not definitely known".
- Study notes on Ac 21:31 and 23:35 both say "about 56 C.E."

The five custody events (Ac 21:27-36, 23:23-35, 24:22-27, 25:1-12, 25:23–26:11) belong to this period. 21:27-36 and 23:23-35 cite 56, and 25:1-12 cites 58. The other two inherit 56-58.

`period:paul-trip-to-rome` now covers Ac 27:1–28:31, matching its c. 58-61 dates. A conflict note records that `gl` labels the trip Ac 23:11–28:31; its `route_gl` still starts at Jerusalem and Caesarea. Event Ac 28:11-16 cites 59 (study note on Ac 28:13: Puteoli "about the year 59 C.E.").

## Citations

```json
{"publication": "it-2", "location": "it-2 p. 205, “LASEA” (WOL ¶3)",
 "url": "https://wol.jw.org/en/wol/d/r1/lp-e/1200002683", "quote": "Lasea is usually identified with …"}
```

- `quote` is optional, copied verbatim, at most 25 words, and must be a substring of the cited page. The app shows the quote and links out. It never reproduces whole articles.
- `location` gives the printed page (taken from WOL `pageNum` markers) plus the WOL paragraph number.
- Map labels (gl "Paul's Travels", nwtsty App. B13) are cited without a quote.

## Conflicts

Disagreements go in `notes[]` with `"kind": "conflict"`. Examples:

- A gazetteer disagrees with Insight distances.
- Two publications word an identification differently.
- Verse ranges overlap.

We record both sides. We never pick one silently.
